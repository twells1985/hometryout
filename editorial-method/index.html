How we test — editorial method

We pick products by sales data, not by personal interest. That is the standing rule for this site.

How a product gets into a guide

  1. Evidence first. We look at what people are actually buying — Amazon best-seller and bought-this-month signals, search trends, and program data — and score products on demand, growth, search intent, competition, commission, conversion, content difficulty, return risk, repeat buying and platform fit.
  2. Verify the product page. Before a product is recommended, its name, model, size and variant are checked on the product page itself. We never invent products, ASINs, prices, specs, reviews, ratings or quotes.
  3. One honest limitation each. Every product card says one thing to watch out for. A guide that only praises products is not a guide.
  4. No prices typed in. Prices change. Buy buttons say "Check price on Amazon" and show the live price on the merchant's page.

What we do not do

  • We do not invent test results or claim to have used a product we have not used. Where a product has not been used yet, the guide says so.
  • We do not publish fake reviews, fake ratings or fake testimonials.
  • We do not use fear to sell. No federal agency forecasts a nationwide blackout in 2026, and our prep guides say so.
  • We do not link to a brand until that brand's affiliate program has approved us. Until then, the product appears only as a clearly marked placeholder.
  • We do not put affiliate links in emails, PDFs, or social posts. Affiliate links live only on this site.

Evidence labels

Claims in our guides come from the manufacturer's own pages, official guidance (for example Ready.gov), or our own scored research. Sources are listed at the bottom of every guide. If a claim cannot be sourced, it does not go in.

Corrections

If something on this site is wrong, email hello@hometryout.com. We will fix it and note the correction on the page.

Photos

Step photos must be real photos that match the step — taken at home, licensed from a free library with credit, or brand press assets with permission. Realistic AI photos, if ever used, are labelled "Illustrative photo (AI-generated)" in the caption and are never passed off as a test. No drawn diagrams or clip art.
